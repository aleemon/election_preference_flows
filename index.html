How the flows were built

  • First preferences and seats for every election come from Adam Carr's Psephos archive (national summaries). Where the archive's party votes didn't add up exactly to its stated formal total, the sum of the party figures is used. For 2025 the archive's "Others" figure was inconsistent and was replaced using the AEC's formal-vote total.
  • 2004–2025: counted flows. The AEC publishes the national and state two-party-preferred flow of preferences by party; those counts are used directly. Parties under 1.2% of the national vote are grouped as "Others". A small Coalition→Labor band appears where Liberal and National candidates ran against each other.
  • 1949–2001: modelled national flows. The AEC series gives the official national two-party-preferred result, but not flows by party. Major-party votes stay with their party; each minor party's votes are split using a typical historical rate (DLP ≈ 15% to Labor, Democrats ≈ 55%, Greens ≈ 75%, One Nation ≈ 45%, others ≈ 50%), then all rates are shifted by one common amount so the totals match the official 2PP. Treat minor-party bands in these years as indicative.
  • 1940–1946: estimates. No national distribution was made; the 2PP figures shown (Labor 50.3%, 58.2%, 54.1%) are widely cited unofficial estimates. 1919–1937: no reliable national 2PP exists. 1901–1917: first past the post, so there were no preferences.
  • State breakdowns use the AEC's flows by state and party, so they are available for 2004–2025 only.
  • Seat-by-seat counts, 2004–2025, are the AEC's distribution of preferences by division. 1901–2001 come from the count-by-count results in Adam Carr's Psephos archive (compiled from AEC and earlier Commonwealth records), parsed for this site. Before 1984 a count normally stopped once a candidate had an absolute majority, so many older seats end with more than two candidates. 123 seats were returned unopposed. The territory members (Northern Territory from 1922, ACT from 1949) are included, although they had limited voting rights in early parliaments. In 1901 South Australia and Tasmania each elected their members statewide; those two contests show first preferences and the members elected. For 16 seats the archive's count could not be read reliably (or a candidate appears to be missing from its candidate table), so only first preferences are shown; in another 15 the archive's totals change between counts by more than 1%, probably transcription errors, and are shown as published with a note. Two deferred elections (Dickson 1993, Newcastle 1998) are recorded in a form that couldn't be read and are not included.
  • Before 1955 some seats were uncontested, which slightly understates the national vote for the parties holding them.
